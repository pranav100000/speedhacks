# I priced the product by feel and it cost me a year

Everyone tells you to charge more. I had read that advice probably two hundred times before I started, and I still launched at $9 a month because $9 felt like a number nobody could be mad at.

Here is what $9 actually buys you as a solo founder. It buys you customers who are price-sensitive by selection. It buys you support requests that cost more than the customer pays in a year. It buys you a growth chart that looks fine and a bank balance that does not.

I raised prices twice. The first time, from $9 to $19, I grandfathered everyone and apologised in the announcement email. I should not have apologised. The second time, from $19 to $49, I did not grandfather anyone, I gave 60 days notice, and I explained what the money was for: me, working on this full time, instead of contracting three days a week to pay rent.

I lost 23% of customers in the second raise. Revenue went up 71%. The people who stayed are the people I actually like talking to, which turns out to matter a lot more than I expected when there is nobody else in the office.

The thing I would tell someone two years behind me is not "charge more". It is this: the price is a filter, and you are choosing who you spend your days with. Choose on purpose.
