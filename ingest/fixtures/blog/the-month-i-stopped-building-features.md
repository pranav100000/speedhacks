# The month I stopped building features

In December I did not ship a single feature. I want to write down what happened, because it was the best month the company has had and I almost did not let it happen.

## The setup

By November the product had 61 paying customers and roughly 40 features I could name off the top of my head. Each one had a story. Someone asked, I said yes, I built it in a weekend. That is the whole playbook for a solo founder: be fast, be close to the customer, say yes.

The problem is that saying yes 40 times produces a product that 61 people each use 10% of, and no two of them use the same 10%.

## What I did instead

I picked the three things people actually did every day, based on the logs, not the emails. Then I spent the month making those three things fast, obvious, and hard to break. I deleted [nine features](https://example.com/changelog/december) outright. I emailed every customer who had ever used one of the nine and told them the date it was going away.

Four people complained. Two of them churned. Both were on the lowest plan.

## What happened

Support volume dropped by about half. Activation, which I define as "did a real thing in the first session", went from 38% to 57%. Two customers upgraded without being asked, and one of them said, in writing, "it finally feels like it knows what it is for".

I do not think this is a universal lesson. I think it is a lesson for people who have been saying yes for a year and are starting to dread opening their own product. If that is you, delete something this week. Pick the one you are most embarrassed by. You already know which one it is.
